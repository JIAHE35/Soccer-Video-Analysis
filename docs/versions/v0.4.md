# V0.4 - Team, Goalkeeper, and Referee Classification

**Status:** Complete

**Git reference:** V0.4 implementation commit

**Milestone:** Classify tracked people as Team A, Team B, either team's
goalkeeper, Referee, or Unknown while preserving V0.3 tracking and ball
behavior.

## Motivation

V0.3 can answer "which bounding boxes belong to the same short-term track?" but
cannot answer "which team or role does this person belong to?" All people are
currently displayed as `player #ID`.

V0.4 adds a role-classification layer after field filtering and tracking, but
before drawing annotations:

```text
YOLO person detection
  -> ByteTrack ID
  -> grass-field filtering
  -> upper-body jersey crop
  -> per-frame color classification
  -> per-track temporal voting
  -> Team A / Team B / Team A GK / Team B GK / Referee / Unknown label
```

Ball detection remains independent at confidence `0.18` and does not receive an
ID.

## Implementation

- Added `src/team_classifier.py` for jersey-region extraction, color features,
  match-specific role classification, and temporal voting.
- Extracts the central upper-body portion of each person box before any
  annotations are drawn.
- Uses configurable HSV masks for red, white, black, blue, and yellow kits. The
  current match defaults are red Team A, white Team B, black referee, and blue
  Team B goalkeeper.
- Supports an optional Team A goalkeeper color. It defaults to `none` because
  that goalkeeper's kit is not clearly established in the source clips.
- Keeps an `Unknown` result when the crop is too small, contains too few useful
  pixels, or has an ambiguous color score.
- Accumulates predictions by ByteTrack ID so that one noisy frame does not
  immediately change a person's displayed team.
- Expires old voting history so reused or stale IDs do not keep an unrelated
  role forever.
- Keeps goalkeepers or ambiguous kits as `Unknown` instead of forcing an
  incorrect label.
- Writes every drawn person and ball detection to CSV using the same frame,
  role, bbox, ID, and confidence shown by the video pipeline.

## Verification results

- Generated a full `1280x588`, 30 FPS, 3534-frame video with a duration of
  117.8 seconds.
- Exported 32,249 detection rows plus the CSV header.
- Observed CSV distribution: 12,446 Team A, 14,146 Team B, 882 Team B
  goalkeeper, 1,179 Referee, 1,786 Unknown, and 1,810 ball rows. Team A
  goalkeeper produced no rows because its kit color is not configured.
- Representative-frame inspection showed clear red/white team separation,
  black-clad referees using the referee label, and the blue goalkeeper using a
  stable Team B GK label. Unconfigured or ambiguous kits remain Unknown.
- Automated tests cover crop boundaries, representative kit colors, ambiguous
  colors, temporal voting, stale tracks, synchronized CSV output, and unchanged
  independent ball behavior.

These counts describe model output, not ground-truth accuracy. Repeated rows for
the same tracked person across frames are counted separately.

## Known risks

- Distant players may have too few jersey pixels for reliable color analysis.
- Shadows, compression, white pitch lines, skin, and overlapping players can
  distort color features.
- Referee and goalkeeper kits may look similar when match colors are configured
  incorrectly.
- ByteTrack ID switches can transfer or reset role history.
- Match-specific color references prioritize reliable results on the current
  video over immediate generalization to every match.

## Out of scope

- Training a custom player/goalkeeper/referee/ball detector.
- Identifying individual players by name or jersey number.
- Reliable identity recovery across camera cuts and replays.
- Continuous ball tracking or interpolation.
- Homography, heatmaps, possession, speed, and distance statistics.

Those items remain candidates for later milestones after V0.4 evaluation.
